# Composition for IMI visuals

The user's supplied 24-second composition video demonstrates a golden-spiral overlay, negative space, leading lines, framing within a frame, and symmetry. These are **ways to organize attention**, not motifs or overlays to reproduce. The video's park, architecture, warm autumn palette, typography, Instagram handles, white diagrams, and drawn lines are not IMI visual requirements. Preserve IMI's separately approved photographic grade and brand layout.

## Choose the image before composing text

1. State the single subject and action: for example, a speedboat accelerating across open island water. Make the vessel the first visual read and keep its silhouette and direction of travel unambiguous.
2. Reserve clean negative space at the upper left for the white logo and a calm, darker area near the bottom for the headline and subheadline. Prefer real sky, water, shade, or depth rather than an empty artificial block. Do not sacrifice the vessel or action to create this space.
3. Use natural leading lines only when the scene offers them: wake, shoreline, horizon, pier edge, ropes, or the vessel's own direction. Let them guide attention toward the boat. Never draw guide lines, arrows, spirals, grids, or blueprint marks into the final image.
4. Use frame-within-frame sparingly when physically plausible: an opening between islands, a foreground rail, shaded canopy edge, or a crew member's viewpoint. Keep the frame secondary to the vessel and avoid needless obstruction.
5. Use symmetry for an appropriate frontal or calm hero view. An accelerating boat often benefits more from asymmetric placement and visible water ahead than forced centering. The golden spiral is a diagnostic for visual flow, not an exact placement rule.
6. Review each of 1080x1350, 1080x1920, and 1080x1080 separately. Keep the key subject, wake/action, upper-left logo area, and lower text area inside the crop. Recompose or generate a separate background if a ratio loses them.

For a real photo, improve composition through crop, straightening, subtle tonal separation, and text placement. Do not invent wake, islands, framing objects, people, or vessel parts just to match a compositional device.

Before delivery, view the result small: can someone identify the vessel/action, then read the headline, then find the logo and URL within a second or two? If the image feels busy, simplify the crop or text treatment rather than adding another compositional graphic.
